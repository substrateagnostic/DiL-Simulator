### notes

**A:** Straight name swap, Diane to Janet. "A woman named Janet" works whether or not Janet is in the room — Skip is introducing her to the board, not to the player. If she's sitting right there, the board can look at her. If she's not, they get the name. The rhythm is identical.

**B:** "Neither does trust" was doing the reader's math. The thirty-one-pound file drawer (two lines later) and the waiting-room callback carry the meaning. "Pen doesn't have an undo button" lands on its own because Parker would stop there — the joke IS the observation, not the moral.

**C:** Replaced the seats-don't-recline and trunk-holds-filing-cabinets reuses with "The parking meter has never expired" — a new specific detail that shows the precision and the something-gone-quiet. He controls the meter the way he controls everything now: perfectly, and at the cost of whatever was left over. Second line ("He runs a clean operation...") unchanged — it's the payoff, not a redundancy.

**D:** The Janitor says the true thing ("the day's done," "stays that way") and lets Andrew do the math about the board meeting. "Tended to" considered but cut for word count — "finished" is cleaner and still caretaker-adjacent. WAIT branch ("The watch has been here since 1947. It'll keep.") uses the horological double meaning: the watch will keep surviving, and the watch keeps time. Reads identically on visit one and visit nine because it's just a fact.

**E1:** The shirt callback is earned — it's Skip's one physical detail of sincerity (ironed for the first time in four years). "He has not stopped" is Willy Loman at his most human: the ritual outlasts the moment. Not promoted to hero. Still pressing a shirt every week because he can't articulate what changed.

**E2:** "The sincere version" is a direct callback to F-TIER-0's "The sincere version. The one he couldn't get to." The player who saw Skip fumble knows exactly what's folded in that pocket. "Never read it aloud" — dry, not maudlin. The saddest card should need no commentary.

**E4:** "STORAGE B" is compatible with both a player who saw the speech and one who didn't. "He has not corrected it" — ambiguous between can't and won't, which is the whole of Skip.

**E5:** He atoned through his vote, not his voice. "Never once explained why" — the reader knows why (the '88 guilt, the chair, the glass of water). Subtext stays sub.

**F ally lines:** Lengths vary — 10, 14, 20, 14, 18 words. Shapes vary — single-word caps payoff (Janet), architectural placement (Diane), semicolon deadpan (Alex), sentence-fragment location (Isaiah), temporal image (Janitor). The Janitor's line avoids the green ledger (already on his dedicated card) and ties him to the 4:55 coffee instead, bridging to the closing line.

**G1:** Cut "their pens" — least visual of the three. Phones (contemporary) and untouched water glasses (callback to the carafe, "the most honest thing in the room") carry more.

**G2:** Cut "their names" — it's generic, and "children's names" implies she obviously knows the parents' names too. The jump from "children's names" to "donuts on Fridays" is the bigger surprise. Both surviving items are specific to Diane's character (families, daily observation).
