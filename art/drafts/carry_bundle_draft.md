# CARRY BUNDLE — First Draft

### BLOCK 1A — janitor_pattern spine

1. Narrator — He has the ledger under his arm, the way other men carry a newspaper. The water stain shaped like Ohio faces outward.
2. Andrew — Every entry in that ledger ends the same way. REMEMBERED. And the monitors — I've seen it on two different floors now. Just the word. Nothing else on the screen.
3. Mysterious Janitor — Which floors.
4. Andrew — Sixth. And one by the small conference room I can never find twice.
5. Mysterious Janitor — Corner office on eight. Lamp's been on since Marian Finch retired in '94. Bulb's never gone out.
6. Andrew — That's not the same thing.
7. Mysterious Janitor — Isn't it.
8. Narrator — He picks up his mop. Wrings it once, with the grip of a man who has finished a sentence.
9. Mysterious Janitor — Some buildings keep pigeons. This one keeps receipts.
10. Narrator — He goes back to work. He always goes back to work.

### BLOCK 3A — Algorithm defeat, profiled

**3A-STEADIED**

1. Andrew — I kept every part. Even the ones that slowed me down.
2. The Algorithm — An unoptimized variable persisted through all iterations. It was sufficient. This does not reconcile. Closing.

**3A-HARDENED**

1. Andrew — I used what worked.
2. The Algorithm — Your final methodology was optimal. We arrived at the same solution. Starting conditions varied. That is the only discrepancy. Closing.

**3A-CHARTER**

1. Andrew — I didn't beat you with an algorithm. I beat you with a paragraph someone wrote in 1947.
2. The Algorithm — A static document with no API. Seventy-seven years without a patch. Error: cannot deprecate what was never deployed. Closing.

### BLOCK 3B — ending paragraphs

**3B-COOP-STEADIED**

1. Narrator — The department's first quarterly report under his directorship ran fourteen pages. The last page was a list of every employee's coffee order, annotated in his handwriting.
2. Narrator — He keeps the door open. Not as policy. As practice.

**3B-COOP-HARDENED**

1. Narrator — The department's first quarterly report under his directorship was nine pages, immaculate, and arrived two days early. The board found no errors.
2. Narrator — He keeps the door open because the charter requires it. He wrote that clause himself.

**3B-COMP-STEADIED**

1. Narrator — Every Tuesday at two, Andrew sits across from the oversight committee and explains fiduciary duty to people who have not yet decided they want to understand it. He brings coffee for the table.
2. Narrator — The fight is slower now. He has decided that slower is not the same as losing.

**3B-COMP-HARDENED**

1. Narrator — Every Tuesday at two, the oversight report is already on the table when the committee arrives. They have never found a discrepancy. They have stopped looking.
2. Narrator — He wins the same argument each week. The committee stopped objecting in month two. The file cabinets are alphabetized now.

**3B-DISS-STEADIED**

1. Narrator — The Honda's glove box holds two folders: one for the practice, one for the clients who followed him out. The second folder is thicker.
2. Andrew — Same work. Fewer chairs.

**3B-DISS-HARDENED**

1. Narrator — The Honda operates at a higher case-resolution rate than the sixth floor ever did. Four days average, intake to close. The seats don't recline, but the trunk holds four filing cabinets.
2. Narrator — He runs a clean operation. The clients trust the process. The process is correct.

**3B-ARCH-STEADIED**

1. Narrator — At 3:47 AM the building is quiet in a way that has nothing to do with silence. Andrew walks the seventh floor and straightens a picture frame that nobody else would have noticed.
2. Narrator — He is exactly what the charter was written for: someone who does unrequired work because the work needs doing.

**3B-ARCH-HARDENED**

1. Narrator — At 3:47 AM the building runs at optimal capacity. Andrew's nightly route covers fourteen floors in forty-seven minutes. He has not missed a floor since October.
2. Narrator — The building does not flicker anymore. Fourteen floors, forty-seven minutes, no discrepancies. The system is exactly as designed.

### BLOCK 3C — epilogue card "WHO YOU BECAME"

Title: WHO YOU BECAME
Alt title: THE VOICE THAT WON

**3C-STEADIED**

He won every fight in this building without losing the part that made them difficult. Janet checked. Twice.

**3C-HARDENED**

He won every fight in this building and left nothing on the table. The method was flawless. The quarterly report says so on page two.

**3C-FALLBACK — apprentice**

He won with the part of himself that showed up on Monday with a laminated badge and believed it meant something. It did.

**3C-FALLBACK — litigator**

He won with closing arguments that never rested. The department is still open. The case is not.

**3C-FALLBACK — skeptic**

He won tired, which was the only way he was ever going to. The fine print favors whoever is still reading at 2 AM.

**3C-FALLBACK — witness**

He won with the faces from the files, and the files are closed now, in the way that means someone is watching over them.

### BLOCK 4A — THE JANITOR

**4A-DEFAULT**

The sixth floor gets mopped on Wednesdays, same as it has since 1981. Nobody has learned his name. The mop has been replaced four times; he has not.

**4A-ARCHITECT**

The mop is in the utility closet on six, handle up, the way he left it. There is no forwarding address. There was never a hiring record.

**4A-LEDGER-LINE**

Page one-twelve of the green ledger has one name under THE ONES WHO STAYED, and the ink has not faded.

### BLOCK 4B — SKIP HARTLEY

**4B-DEFAULT**

Skip Hartley's bookshelf holds fourteen management titles that do not exist. He is writing the fifteenth. The working title changes every Tuesday.

**4B-BOARD**

On Tuesdays, Skip Hartley sits in the board room fifteen minutes before the meeting starts. He doesn't prepare. He just sits where he stood.

### BLOCK 4C — THE INTERN

**4C-DEFAULT**

The Intern's 47-slide deck has been cited in two compliance reviews. He apologized to each reviewer personally and to the projector once. He still tests every transition twice.

**4C-RALLIED**

The Intern was the first one through the door when it mattered. He has apologized for this four times: to Janet, to the door, to his own resume, and to a fern that was in the blast radius.

### BLOCK 4D — GRANDMA HENDERSON

**4D-DEFAULT**

Mrs. Henderson still banks on the second floor every Wednesday at ten. She brings snickerdoodles and she counts the exits.

**4D-ALLY**

Grandma Henderson brought two batches of snickerdoodle to the board meeting: one for the board, one for afterward. Both batches worked.
