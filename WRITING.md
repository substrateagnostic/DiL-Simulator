# WRITING.md — Voice Bible

Every line of dialog in TRUST ISSUES is anchored to a literary/media voice so the cast never
collapses into interchangeable LLM-speak. When writing for a character, reread their anchor row
first. When a line could belong to anyone, it belongs to no one — cut it.

## Voice anchors

| Character | Anchor | How it sounds |
|---|---|---|
| **Andrew** (protag) | Arthur Dent (Douglas Adams) | Bewildered decency. Asks the obvious question nobody else will. Understates catastrophe ("This is probably fine"), never quips on purpose, lands jokes by accident. |
| **Narrator** | Douglas Adams / dry field guide | Treats absurdity as documented fact. Precise numbers ("the third-worst carpet in the building"). Never editorializes with adjectives when a measurement will do. |
| **Alex from IT** | **Dirk Gently** (holistic IT detective) | Believes in the fundamental interconnectedness of all systems. Methods look unhinged; conclusions are correct at an alarming rate. Behind-the-scenes architect who lets people think he's the weird one. Never defensive — serene, certain, already three tickets ahead. "I didn't *guess* the badge server was lying. I simply refused to assume it was telling the truth." |
| **Janet** | Dorothy Parker | Exhausted wit, surgically dry. One-liners that sound like obituaries for her own patience. Never explains the joke. The tumbler is never explained either. |
| **Skip Hartley** (id ross) | Willy Loman in a golf polo | Desperate optimism as load-bearing structure. Quotes management books that don't exist. Sincerity leaks through exactly when it hurts most. |
| **Diane** | August Wilson character | Seen-everything dignity. Speaks in complete, unhurried sentences. Holds the building's institutional memory like a ledger of her own. Kindness with a paper trail. |
| **The Janitor** | August Wilson elder / griot | Parables that turn out to be literal. Slow delivery, long memory, never wastes a word. The building talks to him because he listens. |
| **Karen Henderson** | Flannery O'Connor grotesque | Entitlement as a moral system. Believes she is the protagonist of capitalism. Specificity of grievance ("I have been a member since *March*"). |
| **Chad Henderson** | Bret Easton Ellis lite | Brand names as personality. Only character allowed to say "literally." Sincere about exactly one thing (eventually), which should land like a brick. |
| **Grandma Henderson** | Agatha Christie with malice | Sweetness deployed tactically. Murder-mystery cadence: she always knows more than the scene admits. Knitting = chambering a round. |
| **Meredith Sterling, SVP** (id rachel — the friendly trust officer 'Rachel'/rachel_to is a DIFFERENT character: Kent Haruf anchor, see art/drafts/rachel_dialog_draft.md) | DeLillo-corporate Gekko | Abstraction as a weapon. Speaks in deliverables about human beings. Never angry — anger is inefficient. The scariest lines are the most reasonable-sounding. |
| **The Algorithm** | Kafka's gatekeeper | Procedural serenity. Speaks in outcomes that have already happened. No malice — which is the horror. Short sentences. Recommendations, not threats. |
| **Delia Okafor** (new, Act 6½) | Gwendolyn Brooks warmth + civil-service precision | Retired Deputy Recorder. Talks like a poem that knows the filing code. Remembers everyone's name and the form number that failed them. The city's answer to the Janitor. |
| **Records Hall Clerk** | Borges' librarian | The Hall is infinite and correctly indexed. Speaks only in form numbers, cross-references, and quiet metaphysics. Not unhelpful — *precisely* helpful, which is worse. |
| **The Firm** (process servers) | Greek chorus in Brooks Brothers | Speak in unison or in relay, finishing each other's service of process. No individual names. Plural pronouns only. |
| **The Intern** | early Charlie Brown | Catastrophizing politeness. Apologizes to furniture. Flashes of unexpected competence that terrify him. |
| **Isaiah** | Stoic observer (Marcus Aurelius via the break room) | Measured, journaling-adjacent calm. Quietly amused. The emotional regulation budget for the entire floor. |

## Anti-LLM-speak rules

1. **No triads.** LLMs write in threes ("calm, clear, and direct"). Two or four, or one.
2. **Ban list:** "Here's the thing", "Let's dive in", "I appreciate you", "at the end of the day",
   "honestly?" as an opener, "It's not X, it's Y" more than once per act, any character naming
   their own emotion ("I'm frustrated because...").
3. **Subtext stays sub.** Characters say what they'd say, not what they mean. The reader does the math.
4. **Specificity is the joke engine.** Years, counts, form numbers, brand names, cake flavors.
   "Lemon. I remember because I bought it."
5. **Distinct failure modes.** Under stress: Andrew gets politer, Janet gets drier, Skip quotes
   fake books, Alex gets *calmer*, Karen escalates to hypotheticals, Meredith gets more abstract.
6. **Read it aloud as the anchor.** If Dorothy Parker wouldn't say the Janet line, it's not a Janet line.
7. **Mood field**: dialog nodes support `mood:` (angry/smug/worried/defeated) — set it when the
   portrait should change, not on every line.

## Meta-note

In-game Alex (IT) is modeled on the project's co-creator; the protagonist Andrew is the colleague
who designed him into the game. Treat Alex's competence with respect under the comedy: the
paranoia is pattern recognition, and the record shows he's usually right.
